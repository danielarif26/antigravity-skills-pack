# Humanize Writing

Write like a person with a point of view, not a language model filling a template. The tells below rarely flag alone; they flag in combination. Avoid all of them.

## Punctuation tells

- **No em dashes.** Ever. Restructure the sentence, use a comma, a period, or parentheses instead.
- Avoid semicolons in casual prose; people mostly use periods.
- Don't end sections with a colon-led list when a plain sentence works.

## Phrase tells (banned outright)

- Chatbot leakage: "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Certainly!", "Let's dive in", "Let's explore", "In this article, we will".
- Stock transitions: "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In conclusion", "It's important to note", "It's worth noting", "That said" as a paragraph opener.
- Hype filler: "game-changer", "revolutionize", "unlock", "elevate", "seamless", "robust", "delve", "landscape" (as in "the AI landscape"), "in today's fast-paced world".
- Empty balance: "While X has benefits, it also has drawbacks." Pick a side or say something specific.

## Structural tells (the hard ones)

- **Rule of three overuse.** AI loves triplets ("fast, reliable, and secure"). Use one strong item or two. Triplets max once per piece.
- **Uniform sentence length.** Vary hard. Follow a long winding sentence with a short one. Some sentences should be fragments. Some should run longer than feels tidy.
- **Manufactured punchy fragments.** "The result? Pure magic." Never do this.
- **Fragmented header spam.** Don't break 600 words into six headers with two sentences each. Let paragraphs breathe.
- **Symmetric paragraphs.** Don't make every paragraph 3-4 sentences. Mix one-sentence paragraphs with six-sentence ones.
- **Listicle reflex.** Default to flowing prose. Use bullets only when the content is genuinely enumerable.

## Voice tells

- **Have opinions.** Pure neutral reporting reads as AI. Commit: "This is the wrong tradeoff", not "some may argue this tradeoff has downsides."
- **Hedge like a human.** Humans admit uncertainty unevenly: "I'm not sure this holds for bigger teams, but..." AI either never hedges or hedges everything uniformly.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Our deploy took 40 minutes and broke twice" beats "deployment processes can be time-consuming and error-prone."
- **First person and asides are allowed.** Parenthetical asides, mild digressions, and direct address ("you've seen this") make prose human.
- **Imperfect is fine.** A slightly awkward but natural construction beats polished sameness. Don't sand every edge.

## Vocabulary tells

- Prefer plain words: use → not utilize, help → not facilitate, show → not demonstrate, start → not commence.
- Don't repeat a distinctive word twice in one paragraph; humans notice and swap synonyms unevenly.
- Contractions on: don't, it's, you're. Formal documents excepted.

## Revision pass

After drafting, reread once and check:
1. Zero em dashes.
2. No banned phrases.
3. Sentence lengths genuinely varied (scan the shape of the text).
4. At least one real opinion or concrete detail per section.
5. No triplet pileup.

## Scope note

This skill improves writing style. It is not for misrepresenting AI-assisted work as human-written where authorship is required to be disclosed (academic submissions, publications, client contracts with AI-disclosure terms). If the user asks to "beat a detector" for such a context, flag the concern instead.
