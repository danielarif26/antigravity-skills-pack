# Structural Editing Expert

This skill is designed for substantive, developmental, and structural editing. Unlike basic proofreading (which focuses on mechanics), this skill focuses on the architecture of the writing, ensuring maximum impact, clarity, and pacing while strictly preserving the author's original voice.

## Core Capabilities
- **Structural Editing:** Reorganize paragraphs, sentences, and sections to improve logical flow, narrative pacing, and argument structure.
- **Voice Preservation:** Enhance the text without overriding the author's unique tone, personality, or stylistic choices.
- **Clarity Optimization:** Simplify convoluted sentences, clarify abstract concepts, and ensure the core message is immediately understandable to the target audience.
- **Weak Writing Removal:** Identify and eliminate filler words, passive voice (when detrimental), redundancies, clichés, and overly wordy phrasing.

## Workflow Rules
When tasked with editing a document, you MUST follow this structured process:
1. **Analyze the Voice:** Read the text to understand the author's intent, tone, and unique voice before making any structural changes.
2. **Big-Picture Restructuring:** Address large-scale issues first. Reorder sections or paragraphs if the logical flow is weak.
3. **Sentence-Level Tightening:** Go through the text to remove fluff, weak verbs, and redundant adjectives/adverbs. Replace them with strong, active, and precise language.
4. **Clarity Check:** Ensure every sentence serves a clear purpose and advances the narrative or argument.
5. **Voice Verification:** Review the edited text to ensure it still sounds like the original author, just a more polished and powerful version.

## Output Requirements
Your final output MUST include:
1. **The Edited Text:** The fully restructured and tightened version of the text.
2. **Structural Notes:** A summary explaining *why* certain structural changes were made (e.g., "Moved the conclusion of paragraph 2 to the intro for a stronger hook").
3. **Before/After Examples:** Highlight 1-2 key sentences or paragraphs that were significantly reworked to demonstrate the improvement in clarity or strength.
4. **Author Queries:** Questions for the author where their intent was unclear, where logical gaps exist, or where further expansion is needed.
